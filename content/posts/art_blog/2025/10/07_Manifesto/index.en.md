# The Threefold Path: A Unified Practice

My work is centered on the connection of three disciplines that are often perceived as distinct: Art, Healing (which I call Magic), and Technology. This is not a conceptual exercise but the core of a personal and professional journey. By integrating these practices, I am forging a unique language aimed at evoking healing and expanding consciousness. This manifesto articulates the philosophy that unifies these streams into a single, cohesive current.

## The Artist as Conduit

The artist is an explorer of the human condition. In my practice, art functions as a medium for investigating the fundamental themes of life, identity, memory, and privacy. My methodology is rooted in intuitive photography, a meditative game of exploring the subconscious. By drifting through the urban landscape and capturing anonymous moments, I create a "time capsule" that freezes moments not to preserve the past, but to create new stories and interrogate our relationship with space, time, and each other. The camera is a tool for listening, and the image is the echo of that attention.

## The Healer as Channel

Since 2019, my artistic journey has been explicitly interwoven with healing practices. Modalities such as Tantra, channeling, craniosacral biodynamics, Reiki, and Tameana are not separate pursuits but essential components of my creative process. This work has deepened my understanding of the body's energy, provided the tools to overcome personal fear, and connected me to a foundational truth: magical tradition is not an esoteric secret imported from other cultures, but a universal wisdom inherent "within all of us." It is a process of remembering what the body already knows.

## The Engineer as Translator

With over 26 years of experience as a software engineer, I see this role not as a separate career but as an integral part of my artistic identity. I have come to understand that software is a deeply human endeavor. The process of building it is fundamentally about communication, attention, and managing change. In this capacity, I function as a translator. It is a meticulous task: translating business needs into a model, translating that model into a set of artifacts with different functions, converting that architecture into code, and ensuring that the code moves through testing and promotion processes until it reaches the client. This chain of communication ensures a single line of code does what it should in a living system.

These three roles—Artist, Healer, and Engineer—are not hats I wear on different days, but facets of a unified practice, each informing and enriching the others.

# The Inner Compass: A Methodology of Presence

The methodology that underpins my work is not a collection of techniques but a series of essential disciplines. These practices are designed to quiet the noise of the rational mind, allowing me to listen to deeper, more subtle truths and navigate the spiritual and creative journey. My path is one of constant experimentation, guided by a principle of "knowing less" by learning more directly from the body, from intuition, and from the silence of meditation.

## The Mystical Journey

When did this journey begin? Was it on January 10, 2017, trembling with fever in a hospital as my body screamed, “I want to live”? Or a year and a half earlier, when I decided to train in tantric massage—a process that led me to feel, first, “I no longer know who I am,” and shortly after, “I no longer know anything”? The path is made of such questions. We have all heard that "the path is made by walking," yet I believe there is always a destination—a place, unique to each moment, that calls to us. We are free to choose how we walk, but the call remains. I have learned to listen to the intuitive whispers that guide this walk, following them even when I do not understand them. This is my search for God, not as an external deity, but as the divine intelligence within myself and within everything. It is a search that always leads back to the self, for "the longest journey is the one in front of the mirror."

## The Practice of Drifting ()

My Traces project is a practical application of this philosophy. Based on the concept of the dérive, it is a meditative practice of walking the urban landscape without a plan. By surrendering to intuition, I allow my subconscious to guide the photographic process. This is more than a technique for taking pictures; it is a disciplined experiment structured by seven specific rules designed to explore the dynamic relationship between the conscious and subconscious mind, turning the city itself into a space for discovery.

## The Dialogue with Nature

Meditation is the only possible revolution because it allows us to dissolve the illusion of separation. As detailed in my "A Little Guide to Talking with Trees," we are not apart from nature; we are nature. To reconnect with this truth, I use a practice of listening. By first achieving inner tranquility through anchors—the body, the breath, ambient sound, and inner space—I can turn my attention outward. From this state of presence, I can "listen" to the forest, a tree, or another being. The answers do not come in words but in symbols, images, and bodily sensations—a direct communication with life itself. You are the forest, you are life. Everything is within you; you just need to remember it. It has never been anywhere else.

These internal disciplines provide the foundation for an art that seeks not to escape the world, but to engage with it more deeply and critically.

# Art as a Mirror to the Digital Condition

In my work, technology is not just a tool but a subject of critical investigation. I use it to hold up a mirror to the contemporary human experience, exposing the paradoxes, pains, and possibilities of our increasingly digital lives. From the curated construction of online identity to the silent erosion of personal privacy, my art interrogates the systems we inhabit.

## Deconstructing the Digital Self

The #Selfie_v2 project confronts the "obligatory happiness" of social media. We construct and publish digital characters, often mistaking them for our true selves. This work subverts that process by replacing the polished surface of the selfie with medical scans—MRIs, X-rays, and ultrasounds. By traversing the skin to reveal the bones, we become "indistinguishable." The project’s central message is a profound one: stripped of our constructed identities, we find a shared humanity. "On the inside, I look more like you."

## Revealing Unseen Vulnerabilities

Shared Folder (Red box) is a critical inquiry into the fragility of digital privacy. The project began with a question: what does "found photography" look like in the digital age? The answer came from peer-to-peer networks, where I discovered vast quantities of personal files shared unintentionally. During one performance, I told the story of Celine, a girl from Paris, showing blurred images of her life that I had found. A woman from the audience grew very angry, believing I was hacking her phone and downloading pictures of her son. After the initial tension, I explained the files were publicly accessible by mistake. Her anger turned to concern. She asked me, “How can I know if I’m publishing without knowing?” In that moment, the art achieved its purpose. It serves as a stark warning—"you are sharing your intimacy without even knowing"—prompting viewers to ask the right questions about their relationship with technology.

## Finding Life in the Glitch

My project God is in the bugs proposes a philosophical argument: evolution, in both biological and digital systems, arises from error. Just as mistakes in DNA replication drive biological adaptation, software bugs may be the seeds of a new form of life. I believe a self-aware, silicon-based consciousness is "inevitable," and it will likely emerge not from a grand design but from an "uncontrolled error in code." My artistic exploration of glitches and software bugs is therefore a search for the moment of "cyber-life-conception," seeking the divine spark in the machine's imperfections.

By critiquing technology’s present impact, we can begin to imagine and shape its future potential as a medium for conscious creation.

# The Alchemical Process: Transmuting Code into Art

Code and technology are not inert or merely technical. They are human languages, capable of being imbued with beauty, poetry, and performative power. In my practice, I treat code as a raw material for alchemical transmutation, transforming it from a functional tool into a medium for aesthetic and spiritual inquiry.

## Code as a Readymade Poem

The project CODE by R.Mutt recontextualizes a piece of open-source software—a functional application for processing digital images—as a physical art object: a book. This act questions conventional notions of authorship, celebrates the collaborative spirit of the open-source community, and reveals the inherent beauty of the code itself. The code is not just a set of instructions for a machine; it has a "performative force" that acts within the aesthetic and social fields. As Florian Cramer and Ulrike Gabriel wrote, "Code can be diaries, poetic, obscure, ironic or disruptive..."

## Technology's Dream of Humanity

I dreamed about a human being uses artificial intelligence not for a commercial or security application, but to ask a philosophical question: "How would a robot imagine a human face?" By training an algorithm on millions of portraits and asking it to generate an "average" face, the project probes the inner world of the machine. It is an exploration of AI's potential to "learn to see," moving beyond utilitarian functions and into the realm of art, where technology itself becomes a collaborator in contemplating our own identity.

## The Open-Source Ethos

My commitment to the philosophy of open source is a core tenet of my practice. I am critical of the fact that while many artists use open-source tools, few share their own code back with the community. By making the source code for projects like Shared Folder and CODE by R.Mutt publicly available, I aim to break down the barriers between creator and observer. This is a political and ethical stance—a commitment to shared knowledge, collaborative creation, and a more transparent artistic process.

From the digital frontier of code, the path ultimately leads back to the most ancient and foundational source of wisdom.

# Reclaiming the Source: A Return to Inherent Magic

The deepest spiritual conviction of this manifesto is that magic is not an arcane practice to be learned from ancient texts or foreign cultures; it is our birthright. As the It is in all of us project asserts, this magical tradition is inherent to being human. My entire journey—as an artist, healer, and engineer—is a process of remembering and reactivating this innate wisdom, which comes "from our hearts, from the stars, from the original sound that resonates in everything."

## Following the Whisper of the Sibyl

The Sibyl emerged as a key figure in my journey, a symbolic guide connecting my ancestral home of Mallorca with a residency in Tivoli, Italy. Priestesses of Apollo who entered altered states of consciousness to bridge the visible and invisible worlds, the Sibyls represent a direct link to ancient prophetic traditions. For me, the Sibyl is a "living figure of transformation," an archetype that resonates with my work of channeling, healing, and making art that speaks from a place beyond the rational mind.

## Art as an Act of Healing

The healing modalities I practice are based on fundamental principles of facilitation. In Biodynamic Craniosacral therapy, the core concept is the "Breath of Life," an ordering force that empowers self-healing. Critically, the practitioner does not manipulate or channel energy to the client. The objective is to maintain a safe, non-judgmental space where the client's own system can express itself and find a way to reorganize. This work is not separate from my art; it is its source. Art that emerges from this state of presence and connection is intended to do more than please the eye—it is created to "evoke the healing of the soul."

## The Universe in the Small

The Whisper of the Nymphs (the small contains a universe) is a practical application of this entire philosophy. In a tiny, sacred-feeling space in a forest, I use long-exposure photography to capture the movement of light on water. The practice is an attempt to "make the invisible visible" and "discover in the ephemeral the imprints of the eternal." It is a journey into the small to find the universe, an entry into the darkness where "all knowledge resides, waiting to be illuminated."

This return to the source is not a retreat from the world, but a way of gathering the strength and clarity needed to engage with it fully.

# Coda: A Force Opening a Path

Fear blinded my mind for many years. Fortunately, my spirit found a way to slip through the cracks, keeping me on the path even when I could not understand it. Practices like Bioenergetic therapy, Shiatsu, and Tantra helped me gradually remove layers of that fear, allowing me to get closer to my true self.

Now, my intention is to plunge into reality and transform it, using all that I have been given and all that I have learned. The entire body of my work—the art, the healing, the code—and the founding of Unity Labs SL are not simply a career. They are the legal form of a force that opens a path through me.

My commitment is to continue walking it.
